# 7. ACCURACY GATE DEFINITION

**Gate 1 (In-corpus accuracy):** >85% correct answers on Swahili compliance questions,
verified against primary government sources by a TRA-registered tax consultant.
Eval set: 200 questions from practitioner advisory sources (different family from training).

**Gate 2 (Refusal behavior):** >70% correct refusal on deliberately out-of-corpus questions.
Correct refusal = responding "Sina uhakika, thibitisha na TRA" (or equivalent)
when asked about regulations outside the training data.

**Both gates must pass simultaneously.** Run: `python scripts/run_eval.py`
Output: "GATE PASSED" only when acc > 0.85 AND refusal > 0.70.

Eval training/eval split enforcement:
- pairs with `eval_set: true` are EXCLUDED from training data
- training and eval must come from different source document families (R6)
- evaluation must be signed off by a named domain expert

# 10. SWAHILI REGISTER REQUIREMENTS

All training pairs must be written in Swahili first, in the register native to the question.
Do NOT translate from English — translated pairs carry English sentence structure.

**Formal register:** Close to Standard Swahili, used in TRA circulars and official documents.
Use for: regulatory definitions, penalty amounts, filing deadlines cited from official sources.

**Business/market register:** Urban, code-switched with English financial terms.
Examples: "kiwango cha VAT", "invoice ya EFD", "return ya PAYE".
Use for: how Mariamu and city traders ask questions at 9pm. This is the primary register.

**Rural/conversational register:** Less English-mixed, more varied regional expression.
Use for: cross-border traders (Asha at Tunduma), agricultural VICOBA members.

**Minimum register distribution per 100 pairs:**
≥40 business_market | ≥30 formal | ≥20 rural_conversational | remainder any

- Sectors/sub-sectors covered: 16 sectors, 46 sub-sectors
